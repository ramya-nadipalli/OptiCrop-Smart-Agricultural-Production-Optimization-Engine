Project Overview

What OptiCrop does and why it matters

The OptiCrop project develops an advanced software system that utilizes data-driven insights to optimize agricultural production. By integrating key environmental factors — Nitrogen (N), Phosphorous (P), Potassium (K), soil temperature, humidity, pH, and rainfall — OptiCrop provides intelligent recommendations to farmers for maximizing yields and resource efficiency.

The knowledge and insights gained through OptiCrop can be used by agricultural researchers, policymakers, and stakeholders to advance the understanding of crop-environment interactions and inform evidence-based strategies.

OptiCrop revolutionizes agricultural practices by providing farmers with a smart production optimization engine. By integrating key environmental factors and crop-specific recommendations, it enables farmers to achieve optimal yields, improve resource efficiency, and contribute to sustainable agricultural practices.

About the Data

The 7 input parameters used for crop prediction

N

Nitrogen (N)

Essential macronutrient for chlorophyll production, plant growth, and yield. Higher N values indicate nitrogen-rich soil ideal for leafy crops.

Range: 0 – 140 mg/kg
P

Phosphorus (P)

Vital for root development, energy transfer, and flower/fruit production. Directly affects nutrient uptake efficiency in the plant.

Range: 5 – 145 mg/kg
K

Potassium (K)

Important for water regulation, enzyme activation, and disease resistance. Affects overall plant health and stress tolerance.

Range: 5 – 205 mg/kg

Temperature

Average soil temperature affects seed germination, root growth, and microbial activity. Optimal bioactivity range: 50–75°F (10–24°C).

Range: 8°C – 44°C

Humidity

Relative humidity influences plant transpiration, fungal disease risk, and crop water requirements throughout the growing season.

Range: 14% – 100%
pH

pH Level

Indicates soil acidity or alkalinity. Acidic: pH < 7, Neutral: pH = 7, Basic: pH > 7. Strongly affects nutrient availability.

Range: 3.5 – 9.9

Rainfall

Total annual precipitation is crucial for crop selection and irrigation planning. Determines water availability and stress resistance requirements for the crop. Different crops have very different rainfall requirements — rice needs much more than maize, for example.

Range: 20 – 300 mm

Machine Learning Approach

How the prediction model was built and validated

01

Data Visualization & Analysis

Performed univariate, bivariate, and multivariate analysis using Matplotlib and Seaborn to understand relationships between soil parameters and crop types. Identified key correlations and data distributions.

PandasMatplotlibSeabornEDA
02

Data Preprocessing

Applied log transformation on the Potassium (K) values to normalize the skewed distribution. Split data into training and test sets. Ensured no missing values or outliers affected model training.

Scikit-learnLog TransformNumPy
03

Model Building

Implemented a Logistic Regression classifier using Scikit-learn. Logistic Regression was chosen for its interpretability, speed, and strong performance on this type of multi-class classification problem.

Logistic RegressionMulti-classScikit-learn
04

Model Evaluation

Evaluated using accuracy score, confusion matrix, and classification report. Achieved over 95% accuracy on the test set. Model was serialized using Pickle for deployment in the Flask web application.

95% AccuracyConfusion MatrixPickle
05

Web Deployment

Deployed the model as a Flask web application. Users submit soil and climate data through an HTML form, the backend processes it through the model, and the recommended crop is returned instantly.

PythonFlaskHTML/CSSJinja2
